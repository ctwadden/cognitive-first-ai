# Try → Check → Explain
A ten-minute evidence check • High school • No AI account needed

## Your goal
Explain what a small dataset supports and what it does not prove.

## The fictional data
These are invented groups for a reasoning exercise. They are not real students or research findings. Each row represents the average for a different group; we do not know group sizes or how the groups were chosen.

Group A: 2 hours of daily recreational screen time; average quiz score 78%.
Group B: 4 hours of daily recreational screen time; average quiz score 72%.
Group C: 6 hours of daily recreational screen time; average quiz score 66%.

## 1. TRY — 3 minutes
Read the three rows. Write one sentence describing the pattern.

My sentence: __________________________________________________________

What else would you want to know before giving advice based on this table?

My question: __________________________________________________________

STOP. Keep the sample answer below covered until your teacher tells you to reveal it.

## 2. CHECK — 4 minutes
This is a deliberately flawed answer written for this activity, not an actual AI transcript:

“The table proves that screen time causes lower scores. If every student cuts screen time by two hours, their quiz score will rise by exactly six percentage points.”

Work with a partner. Underline the words that claim more than the table can support.

A. What pattern does the table actually show?
B. Why might the cause be uncertain? Give one possible alternative explanation.
C. Rewrite the answer in one or two careful sentences. Include one numerical comparison.

## 3. EXPLAIN — 3 minutes
Cover the sample answer and your pair’s correction. You may still look at the data.

In two sentences, explain why “these groups differ” is a stronger conclusion than “this change will improve everyone’s score.” Name one thing you would investigate next.

Or explain aloud to your teacher, using the data to support your answer.

## If you finish early
A headline says: “One change guarantees better grades.” Write a more responsible headline for this fictional table.

## Reflection
What did you change after checking the claim? What part of the reasoning was yours?
