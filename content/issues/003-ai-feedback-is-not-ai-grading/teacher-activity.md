# Comment, Confirm, Decide

**12 minutes · Grades 9–12 · Paper and pen · No AI account needed**

## Your goal

Decide what a feedback comment can tell you about a piece of work, then provide your own evidence of understanding.

The town, data and student response below are fictional. The feedback is a prepared AI-style example, not feedback produced from a real student's work.

**Fictional source information**

Riverton installed shade canopies at four bus stops. Before installation, a town worker recorded an average afternoon surface temperature of 34°C at those stops. After installation, the worker recorded an average of 29°C. The readings were taken on different days. The town did not record cloud cover, wind or the temperature at bus stops without canopies.

**Prepared student response**

“The shade canopies caused the bus stops to become exactly 5°C cooler. The temperatures prove the plan worked.”

**Prepared AI-style feedback**

“Strong answer. You used the temperature data accurately and clearly showed that the canopies were effective. Add one sentence explaining why shade lowers temperature.”

1. Underline one part of the feedback that is useful for improving the written response.

2. Circle one part of the feedback that claims more than the source information can support.

3. Complete this sentence: “From the written response, the feedback can tell us __________, but it cannot tell us whether the writer __________.”

4. Revise the prepared student response so that its claim matches the available evidence. Write two or three sentences.

5. Without looking back at your revision, answer this fresh question: What is one additional piece of evidence Riverton should collect before deciding that the canopies caused the temperature change? Explain why that evidence would help.

## Exit question

Which part of your work gives the teacher the best evidence of your understanding: your revision or your answer to the fresh question? Explain your choice in one sentence.
